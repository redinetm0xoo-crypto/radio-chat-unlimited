Open bands near you

Lines on the map show stations heard by, or hearing, stations in your grid square in the last 15–30 minutes (FT8/FT4 etc. from PSK Reporter, WSPR from wspr.live). Guide from signal reports: best SNR > 10 dB = SSB likely, > −1 dB = CW, otherwise digital modes only. No spots doesn't always mean closed – it may just be quiet. Data: PSK Reporter (Philip Gladstone, N1DQ) and wspr.live. Refreshes at most every 5 minutes.

What the numbers mean

  • SFI (solar flux): higher is better for HF – 70 poor, 100+ good, 150+ excellent.
  • SN (sunspot number): more sunspots = better upper-HF propagation.
  • A / K index: geomagnetic activity – lower is better. K 0–2 quiet, 3–4 unsettled, 5+ storm.
  • X-ray: flare class A/B/C quiet, M/X can cause HF blackouts on the daylit side.
  • Solar wind / Bz: fast wind (>500 km/s) with negative Bz = more disturbance and aurora.
  • Aurora / Aur Lat: activity level and the lowest latitude the aurora reaches.
  • Sig noise: expected background noise in S-units.
  • Grey line: the twilight band – low-band DX often peaks along it.
